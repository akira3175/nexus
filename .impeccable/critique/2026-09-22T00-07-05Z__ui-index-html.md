## Design Specificity Verdict

Nexus feels authored as a restrained editorial watch boutique. The warm-black and gold palette, centered serif wordmark, oversized hero title, framed product photography, and serif/sans pairing form a coherent luxury language. This specificity weakens after the homepage: detail, cart, checkout, and invoice inherit the visual system but use conventional structures. Without the watches, the shell could still plausibly serve jewelry, fragrance, or menswear.

The deterministic scan returned zero findings, but it ran in degraded regex mode because HTML/CSS parser modules were unavailable. It did not evaluate custom properties, selector matching, or computed contrast, so this is an undercount rather than a clean bill of health. Browser rendering and visual overlays were unavailable because no in-app browser backend was present.

## Overall Impression

The visual foundation is good and the functional demo is unusually complete. The biggest opportunity is to extend the confidence of the hero into the full shopping journey through persistent cart feedback, stronger color hierarchy, factual product storytelling, and actionable form errors.

## What's Working

1. A coherent luxury visual world built from restrained color, serif display typography, generous spacing, square geometry, and strong watch imagery.
2. Thoughtful semantic and accessibility groundwork: landmarks, labels, status and alert roles, focus rings, reduced-motion handling, alt text, and print treatment.
3. A complete and honest demo journey covering search, product fallbacks, cart persistence, quantity controls, checkout validation, invoice validation, empty states, and printing.

## Priority Issues

### P1: Cart state is invisible outside the cart

After adding a watch, users receive only local confirmation. Add a persistent header count such as `Giỏ hàng (1)` and update it after add, remove, and quantity changes. Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`.

### P1: Premium storytelling stops at the hero

The imagery promises an authored watch brand, while product copy communicates mostly attributes. Add concise, factual narratives grounded in silhouette, finish, movement, dimensions, and use context. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.

### P2: The entire interface speaks in gold

Gold carries branding, headings, navigation, links, prices, body copy, and controls. Reserve bright gold for brand, active states, price, and primary interactions; move body copy toward warm ivory or stone. Suggested command: `$impeccable colorize`.

### P2: Checkout errors are global rather than actionable

The aggregate error does not identify the exact invalid field or move focus. Add per-field error text, `aria-describedby`, invalid styling, and focus the first invalid control. Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`.

### P2: Demo disclosure repeatedly interrupts the journey

The prototype caveat appears at many stages. Use one compact persistent demo status and retain only context-specific disclosures at checkout and invoice. Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, first-time shopper:** No durable cart count after add; the sole COD option looks like a choice; aggregate checkout errors make recovery uncertain.

**Minh, mobile price-conscious shopper:** Two-column cards may become dense at 320-420 px; `Xem tất cả` consumes attention before filtering; the invoice table depends on horizontal scrolling without a swipe affordance.

**Alex, experienced ecommerce user:** Expects a global cart count, quick add or a faster path after adding, and undo after removing a row.

## Minor Observations

- The homepage CTA is generic despite the products being directly below.
- Active navigation is weak on detail, checkout, and invoice pages.
- One sprite sheet keeps assets efficient but limits richer detail-page photography.
- Hover states are good, while visited or selected differentiation is limited.
- The hero animation respects reduced motion but is not especially watch-specific.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the next pass prioritize commerce clarity, visual hierarchy, or brand storytelling?
- Should Nexus remain restrained editorial luxury, or become warmer and more product-led?
- Should the next implementation cover only the top three issues or the full flow?
